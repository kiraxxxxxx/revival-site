01 — REVIVAL
壱 Who We Are

A marketplace and execution platform for industrial revival — not a single factory, and not a single deal.

Where a distressed but fundamentally valuable industrial asset exists, REVIVAL brings together the capital, operator, buyer and strategic partners required to return it to productive use. Beximco is the first large-scale demonstration of that model — the proof of concept, not the definition of what REVIVAL is.

REVIVAL is not simply the "front face" of Beximco

REVIVAL is an independent platform and fashion brand bringing together international/NRB capital, government facilitation, financial institutions, international operators, Japanese partners, global buyers and local industrial assets — with REVIVAL's own management and commercial platform coordinating all of it. If the Beximco initiative succeeds, the same model is designed to be repeated across other distressed industrial assets in Bangladesh.

Three countries, one operating platform.

REVIVAL is not a single-country operation. The platform is built on three capabilities that rarely sit together in one company — and the combination is what makes the model work.

Japan

Design and quality philosophy

The discipline, the standards and the long-term thinking Japanese buyers require. REVIVAL was founded here, out of research at Kyushu University, and is registered in Fukuoka.

Bangladesh

Manufacturing and craftsmanship

Industrial-scale garment production alongside living craft traditions — Nakshi Kantha, Jamdani, Khadi — that no other production country can offer at the same time.

China

Manufacturing and operational expertise

Production engineering and operational discipline, brought into the platform through REVIVAL's joint venture with Besting.

REVIVAL is also a buyer, not only a platform

Where commercially appropriate, REVIVAL acts as a brand and buyer in its own right — placing orders into revived facilities and creating the demand that makes a reopening sustainable, rather than waiting for third-party orders to appear.

02 — INDUSTRIAL CAPABILITY
弐 The Industrial Revival Initiative

Beximco: 320 acres, twelve garment units, and the first proof that this model works.

Bangladesh's largest vertically integrated garment complex — built to supply H&M, Zara, Marks & Spencer, PVH, Next and Target — stalled in the early 2020s under pandemic demand shocks and frozen trade finance. Our origin fiction, written for the Social Fiction Design Competition's "World of Three Zeros," imagines Dhaka in 2050: a river runs clear again, and Fabric Credits have become a circular currency. It's a narrative framework, not a financial projection — the underlying instrument, Fabric Credit, is a real, verifiable unit tied to diverted textile waste, structured for corporate Scope 3 reporting — the kind of instrument Japanese manufacturers and retailers increasingly need for their own reporting.
